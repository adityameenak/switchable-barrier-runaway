Switchable thermal barriers: cool in use, a firewall in runaway

A barrier between battery cells has two jobs that pull in opposite directions. Conducting heat keeps cells cool in normal use but passes a runaway cell's heat to its neighbours. Insulating stops propagation but traps heat every day. A barrier that conducts below a switch temperature and insulates above it does both. This page shows the simulation results behind that claim.

Boundary

The contained region is bounded by a constant hot-state conductance koff/L. The runaway cell must lose heat to the cooled end (h = ) faster than it leaks through the barrier.

All or nothing
–

Every run ends with either 1 or all 5 cells in runaway. Once cell 2 goes, each later cell faces a trigger at least as severe, so the barrier has to stop the first hand-off.

Default design
–

The default barrier (2 mm, koff = 0.05 W/m·K) sits inside the boundary, but not by much. A real design would want more margin.

Method and validation

How the numbers were made

Model

ρcp ∂T/∂t = ∂/∂x( k(T) ∂T/∂x ) + qrxn dα/dt = keff(T)(1 − α), qrxn = ρcp ΔTad dα/dt keff = karrkmax/(karr+kmax), karr = A e−Ea/RT ln kbarrier = ln koff + ln(kon/koff)/(1+e(T−Tsw)/w)

Finite volumes with harmonic-mean face conductances. Backward-Euler conduction with k(T) lagged, solved as a tridiagonal system. An exact exponential update for the reaction, coupled by operator splitting. Adaptive steps while a cell ignites. Energy is tracked every step; the worst residual across all runs is of the total.

Validation tests (pytest, run on every push)

✓Insulated stack, no reaction: total energy constant to 10−12, even across the nonlinear switch.
✓Steady conduction through cell | barrier | cell matches the series-resistance solution to 10−8 K.
✓A single adiabatic reacting node ends at exactly T0 + ΔTad, and ignites within 1 % of a stiff ODE solver.
✓Propagation time converges under grid and time-step refinement (shown right).

The convergence test caught a modelling problem. Extended to a 900 °C burning cell, the onset-fitted Arrhenius rate reaches about 109 s−1. That makes the reaction front around 0.1 µm thick, so no practical mesh resolves it and the predicted time never settles as the mesh is refined. Capping the rate at kmax = 1 s−1, standing in for the transport limits real decomposition has, gives a 0.6 mm front and a converged answer.

Limitations

  • 1D: heat only flows through the stack thickness, and the cooled ends are the only heat sink. With weak end cooling (h ≲ 30 W/m²·K) no barrier stops propagation in this model.
  • Single-step lumped kinetics with a rate ceiling. Real runaway involves several overlapping reactions.
  • No venting, ejecta, gas flow, radiation or contact resistance.
  • An idealised switch: instant, reversible, with no hysteresis or wear.
  • All parameters are illustrative, as noted at the top.
